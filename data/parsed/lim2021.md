## Lipid signalling enforces functional specialization of T reg cells in tumours

Accepted: 12 January 2021

Published online: 24 February 2021

Regulatory T cells (T reg cells) are essential for immune tolerance 1 , but also drive immunosuppression in the tumour microenvironment 2 . Therapeutic targeting of T reg cells in cancer will therefore require the identification of context-specific mechanisms that affect their function. Here we show that inhibiting lipid synthesis and metabolic signalling that are dependent on sterol-regulatory-element-binding proteins (SREBPs) in T reg cells unleashes effective antitumour immune responses without autoimmune toxicity. We find that the activity of SREBPs is upregulated in intratumoral T reg cells. Moreover, deletion of SREBP-cleavage-activating protein (SCAP)-a factor required for SREBP activity-in these cells inhibits tumour growth and boosts immunotherapy that is triggered by targeting the immune-checkpoint protein PD-1. These effects of SCAP deletion are associated with uncontrolled production of interferon-γ and impaired function of intratumoral T reg cells. Mechanistically, signalling through SCAP and SREBPs coordinates cellular programs for lipid synthesis and inhibitory receptor signalling in these cells. First, de novo fatty-acid synthesis mediated by fatty-acid synthase (FASN) contributes to functional maturation of T reg cells, and loss of FASN from Treg cells inhibits tumour growth. Second, T reg cells in tumours show enhanced expression of the PD-1 gene, through a process that depends on SREBP activity and signals via mevalonate metabolism to protein geranylgeranylation. Blocking PD-1 or SREBP signalling results in dysregulated activation of phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase in intratumoral T reg cells. Our findings show that metabolic reprogramming enforces the functional specialization of T reg cells in tumours, pointing to new ways of targeting these cells for cancer therapy.

The essential roles of immunometabolism in regulating cell state and fate are beginning to be unravelled 3,4 , but context-dependent metabolic effects in vivo are underexplored. In particular, how T reg cells rewire their metabolic programs to enforce functional adaptation in tumours remains unclear, despite the identification of metabolic pathways supporting the function of T reg cells under homeostatic conditions 4,5 . To explore the molecular basis for the functional adaptation of T reg cells in tumours, we performed transcriptome analysis of T reg cells isolated from tumour and peripheral tissues after challenging wild-type mice with B16 melanoma cells. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) using curated metabolic pathways (Supplementary Table 1) revealed that pathways related to lipid metabolism were among the top enriched pathways in intratumoral T reg cells compared with those from peripheral lymph nodes (PLNs) (Fig. 1a). In particular, gene targets for SREBPstranscription factors that promote de novo synthesis of lipids, including fatty acids and cholesterol (Extended Data Fig. 1a) 6 -were the most enriched gene signatures (Fig. 1a and Extended Data Fig. 1b). Ingenuity pathway analysis also revealed that SREBP1 (encoded by Srebf1 ) and SREBP2 (encoded by Srebf2 ) were among the top enriched upstream transcriptional regulators in intratumoral T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 1c). Such enrichment was further validated in public single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) datasets of mouse melanoma models 7,8 (Extended Data Fig. 1d, e) and individuals with breast cancer 9 (Extended Data Fig. 1f) or head and neck squamous cell carcinoma 10 (HNSCC; Extended Data Fig. 1g), indicating upregulation of the SREBP gene signature in T reg cells from the tumour microenvironment (TME).

We next explored whether SREBP signalling in T reg cells is affected by other inflammatory signals. We first analysed a public transcriptome dataset of activated T reg (aT reg ) cells in an acute inflammation model 11 , and found that SREBP gene targets were not enriched in aT reg cells compared with resting T reg (rT ) cells (Extended Data Fig. 1h). scRNAseq analysis of T reg cells from the central nervous system (CNS) of mice with experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) also did not reveal increased expression of SREBP gene targets (Extended Data Fig. 1i, j). Although glucose metabolism supports the proliferation and survival of T reg cells in tumours 12 , intratumoral T reg cells did not increase glucose uptake compared with their splenic counterparts (Extended Data Fig. 1k). Instead, glucose uptake was upregulated in T reg cells from EAE mice (Extended Data Fig. 1l). These results reveal context-specific alteration of the SREBP signature in T reg cells from the TME.

To determine the functional importance of SREBP signalling in T reg cells, we crossed mice with loxP -flanked Scap alleles with Foxp3 YFP-Cre mice (generating Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice) to delete Scap specifically in T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 1m). Deletion of SCAP inhibited the expression of SREBP gene targets in intratumoral T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 1n). We next challenged Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice with models of MC38 colon adenocarcinoma and B16 melanoma. Although MC38 colon adenocarcinoma showed progressive growth in control mice, Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice showed a rapid and complete clearance of tumour cells (Fig. 1b). In addition, the growth of B16 melanoma was substantially reduced in Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice (Fig. 1c). To explore the potential of targeting SCAP/SREBP signalling for treating established tumours, we generated Foxp3 GFP-Cre-ERT2 Scap fl/fl Rosa26 YFP mice in order to acutely delete Scap in T reg cells upon treatment with tamoxifen 13,14 when the tumour started to appear or was fully established. Under both treatment regimens, tumour growth was reduced in Foxp3 GFP-Cre-ERT2 Scap fl/fl Rosa26 YFP mice (Fig. 1d, e), whereas no difference in tumour growth was found without tamoxifen treatment (Extended Data Fig. 1o). We next explored the combinatorial effect of T reg -cell-specific SCAP deletion with anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. Although the growth of B16 melanoma was largely refractory to anti-PD-1 treatment 15 , deletion of SCAP in Treg cells  sensitized  the  mice  to  this  immunotherapy  (Fig.  1f). These results from various models indicate that targeting SCAP/SREBP signalling in T reg cells unleashes potent antitumour effects.

Treg cells  are  essential  in  maintaining  immune homeostasis 1 . Compared with control mice, Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice had normal cellularity and effector/memory populations (CD44 hi CD62L lo ) of conventional CD4 + and CD8 + T cells in the spleen and PLNs (Extended Data Fig. 2a, b), as well as inflammatory cytokine production (Extended Data Fig. 2c, d). Accordingly, Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice did not show histological abnormality in multiple organs examined, except for minor inflammation in the pancreas (Extended Data Fig. 2e). In addition, levels of serum anti-nuclear antibodies (ANAs) and anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA) antibodies were not elevated (Extended Data Fig. 2f, g), and lifespan was normal (Extended Data Fig. 2h). Thus, SCAP activity is largely dispensable for supporting the function of T reg cells in maintaining self-tolerance under steady state.

Moreover, upon induction of EAE disease, Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice showed no alterations in disease scores (Extended Data Fig. 3a), CNS inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 3b), or T cell infiltration and cytokine production (Extended Data Fig. 3c, d). We also generated female Foxp3 Cre/DTR Scap fl/fl or control mosaic mice. In these mice, owing to the expression of Foxp3 on the X chromosome, Treg cells express either Cre recombinase (which mediates Scap deletion) or the diphtheria toxin receptor (DTR); the T reg cells that express the DTR can be depleted through the use of diphtheria toxin, in turn driving the activation of the remaining SCAP-deficient or control T reg cells to enforce immunosuppression 16,17 . In this model of acute inflammation, Foxp3 Cre/DTR Scap fl/fl mice showed normal cellularity, activation and cytokine production of conventional CD4 + and CD8 + T cells (Extended Data Fig. 3e-h), suggesting the intact suppressive function of SCAP-deficient T reg cells. Altogether, loss of SCAP/SREBP signalling in T reg cells suppresses tumour growth without discernible autoimmune or inflammatory consequences.

To determine the effects of SCAP-deficient T reg cells in shaping the TME, we used scRNAseq to unbiasedly profile CD45 + immune cells in mice bearing B16 tumours (Fig. 2a). Compared with control mice, there were increased proportions of total and effector/memory-phenotype CD8 + T cells and total Foxp3 -CD4 + T cells in the TME from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice (Fig. 2b), as verified by flow-cytometry analysis (Extended Data Fig. 4a, b), including increased numbers of CD8 + T cells with effector (CD44 hi CD62L lo ; T eff ) and central memory (CD44 hi CD62L hi ; T cm ) but not exhaustion (Tim3 + PD-1 + ; T ex ) phenotypes (Extended Data Fig. 4c-e). There was also an increase in the frequency of conventional CD8 + and CD4 + T cells producing tumour necrosis factor-α (TNFα) (and a trending increase in cells producing interferon-γ, IFNγ) from tumours but not PLNs (Extended Data Fig. 4f, g). The ratio of CD8 + T cells versus Treg cells-a key prognostic factor for cancer 2 -was also increased in Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl tumours (Fig. 2c). Moreover, while MC38 colon adenocarcinoma was rejected in Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice (Fig. 1b), depletion of CD8 + T cells partially restored tumour growth (Extended Data Fig. 4h), indicating a partial dependence on functions of CD8 + T cells for these effects.

Furthermore, scRNAseq analysis identified a reduced proportion of Treg cells in tumours from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice (Fig. 2b). Flow-cytometry analysis also revealed a reduced frequency of T reg cells in tumours but not PLNs from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice (Extended Data Fig. 5a), whereas the number of intratumoral Treg cells was not reduced (Extended Data Fig. 5a), probably because of the increase in the total number of tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (Extended Data Fig. 5b). In contrast with tumours, there were no changes in the accumulation of T reg cells under steady state (Extended Data Fig. 5c).

We next determined the effects of SCAP deficiency on the proliferative state of T reg cells. Consistent with the requirement for SCAP-mediated cholesterol biosynthesis in the proliferation of CD8 + T cells upon acute antigen stimulation 18 , SCAP-deficient T reg cells showed defective proliferation in vitro, which was partially reversed by cholesterol supplementation (Extended Data Fig. 5d). However, Treg cells in tumours or PLNs from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice did not exhibit altered incorporation of the thymidine analogue BrdU (Extended Data Fig. 5e), and SCAP-deficient peripheral T reg cells also showed normal lymphopaenia-induced proliferation (Extended Data Fig. 5f) and proliferation induced by treatment with interleukin-2 (IL-2)/ anti-IL-2 complex 19,20 (Extended Data Fig. 5g). Moreover, expression of cytotoxic-T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA4), which is closely associated with metabolism-driven proliferation 21 , was not altered by SCAP deficiency (Extended Data Fig. 5h, i). Therefore, SCAP function is largely dispensable for the proliferation of T reg cells under homeostasis and in the TME, highlighting a context-specific proliferative requirement as observed in CD8 + T cells 18 .

We then examined the apoptosis of Treg cells, and found that SCAP-deficient T reg cells showed increased staining of active caspase-3 (a protein that triggers apoptosis) in tumours but not in PLNs (Extended Data Fig. 5j). Increased cell death due to SCAP deficiency was also observed in a cell-intrinsic system of female Foxp3 Cre/+ Scap fl/fl mosaic mice that contained Scap -deficient and Scap -sufficient T reg cells 19 ; this increased apoptosis was associated with a reduced frequency and a trending numerical reduction of intratumoral T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 5k-m). However, expression of CD36 (which mediates the lipid uptake needed for the survival of intratumoral T reg cells 22 ) as well as uptake of neutral lipids were unaffected by SCAP deficiency (Extended Data Fig. 5n, o). Control and SCAP-deficient T reg cells also showed comparable mitochondrial profiles, including mitochondrial mass, membrane potential, and production of reactive oxygen species (Extended Data Fig. 5p). Thus, SCAP is dispensable for lipid uptake and mitochondrial fitness but contributes to the survival of intratumoral Treg cells.

We next explored the possible involvement of SCAP in the differentiation of T reg cells from quiescent rT reg (CD44 lo CD62L hi ) cells to an aTreg (CD44 hi CD62L lo ) population 1,23 and the maintenance of Foxp3 expression 1 , both of which are shaped by inflammatory conditions. Under steady state, the accumulation of aT reg cells was unchanged in the absence of SCAP (Extended Data Fig. 5q). In addition, the percentage and number of aT reg cells amongst intratumoral SCAP-deficient T reg cells were normal (Extended Data Fig. 5r). Moreover, Foxp3 expression was comparable in control and SCAP-deficient intratumoral T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 6a, b), as well as in T reg cells upon transfer into Rag1 -/-mice 24 (Extended Data Fig. 6c). These results indicate a dispensable role of SCAP in the accumulation of aT reg cells and expression of Foxp3.

Intratumoral T reg cells can become fragile-a unique cellular state marked by retention of Foxp3 expression but aberrant IFNγ production 25,26 . We found that IFNγ-expressing T reg cells were unaltered by SCAP deficiency at steady state (Extended Data Fig. 6d), but there was an increased frequency of IFNγ + T reg cells in tumours but not PLNs from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice compared with controls (Fig. 2d). scRNAseq analysis also revealed that macrophages in tumours from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice had elevated expression of the IFNγ-inducible immunomodulatory genes B2m , H2-Aa and Cd274 (ref. 27 ; Extended Data Fig. 6e). Moreover, the growth of both B16 melanoma and MC38 colon adenocarcinoma was substantially inhibited in female Foxp3 Cre/+ Scap fl/fl mosaic mice (Fig. 2e, f), in line with the dominant effect of fragile T reg cells in reshaping the TME 25,26 . In the aforementioned tamoxifen-inducible system (Fig. 1d), both SCAP-deficient and -sufficient T reg cells showed a trending increase in the frequency of IFNγ + T reg cells in tumours but not PLNs from Foxp3 GFP-Cre-ERT2 Scap fl/fl Rosa26 YFP mice (Extended Data Fig. 6f), associated with substantially increased frequencies of IFNγ + and TNFα + CD8 + and Foxp3 -CD4 + T cells (Extended Data Fig. 6g, h). Together, these results show that SCAP/SREBP signalling is essential for maintaining the functional integrity of T reg cells in the TME.

However, deletion of FASN in T reg cells inhibited the growth of MC38 and B16 tumours (Fig. 3c, d). Like the effects of SCAP deletion, these effects were restricted to the TME, as EAE disease score (Extended Data Fig. 7j) and CNS inflammation (Extended Data Fig. 7k) were comparable between control and Foxp3 Cre Fasn fl/fl mice. Similarly, the proportion of CD8 + T cells producing TNFα, although not IFNγ, was increased in Foxp3 Cre Fasn fl/fl mice in tumours specifically (Extended Data Fig. 8a); this was not observed for conventional CD4 + T cells (Extended Data Fig. 8b). However, in contrast with Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice, tumours isolated from Foxp3 Cre Fasn fl/fl mice showed largely normal cellularity and activation state of conventional CD8 + and CD4 + T cells (Extended Data Fig. 8c-f). Moreover, FASN deletion did not alter the accumulation of intratumoral Treg cells, the ratio of CD8 + T cells to T reg cells, or the production of IFNγ by T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 8g-i).

To profile the functional state of FASN-deficient T reg cells in tumours, we performed transcriptome analysis of intratumoral T reg cells, and found that the pathway for fatty-acid metabolism was downregulated in FASN-deficient T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 8j). Of note, the Treg -cell-specific gene signature that is driven by the activation of T cell antigen receptors (TCRs) 29 was also downregulated (Extended Data Fig. 8k). We hypothesized that FASN signalling contributes to TCR-dependent activation of Treg cells and/or functional maturation 29,30 . Indeed, whereas freshly isolated FASN-deficient T reg cells had normal suppressive function in vitro (Extended Data Fig. 8l), following TCR-induced activation 21 , FASN-deficient activated T reg cells had reduced function (Fig. 3e). To link FASN with fatty-acid synthesis, we added exogenous palmitate, a product of FASN, and found that it restored the suppressive function of FASN-deficient activated Treg cells (Fig. 3e). In addition, despite normal TCR-induced proliferation (Extended Data Fig. 8m), FASN deletion impaired the TCR-dependent upregulation of Treg activation and maturation markers, including GITR and CD44 (ref. 30 ; Extended Data Fig. 8n). GITR and CD44 were also reduced on FASN-deficient T reg cells in tumours, but not in PLNs (Extended Data Fig. 8o). These data collectively indicate that FASN signalling contributes to TCR-induced functional maturation of T reg cells.

We next explored additional mechanisms that may act downstream of SCAP/SREBP activation. Our scRNAseq analysis revealed that intratumoral SCAP-deficient T reg cells had reduced expression of Pdcd1 (which encodes PD-1) (Extended Data Fig. 9a). Flow-cytometry analysis also showed that PD-1 expression was substantially reduced on SCAP-deficient T reg cells in tumours, but not in PLNs (Fig. 3f). By contrast, SCAP-deficient T reg cells had normal expression of the T reg signature molecules CTLA4, ICOS, CD69, CD25, Nrp-1, CD39 and CD73 (Fig. 3f and Extended Data Fig. 9b). In addition, PD-1 expression on T reg cells was unaltered in the spleen or non-lymphoid organs from Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl mice under steady state (Extended Data Fig. 9c). Thus, SCAP/SREBP signalling is required for PD-1 expression by T reg cells in tumours, but not in the peripheral lymphoid and non-lymphoid tissues. PD-1 has been shown to dampen signalling via phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase (PI3K) 31 , and PI3K activation is associated with elevated production of IFNγ by Treg cells 24,25 . Consistent with a previous report 25 , anti-PD-1 treatment increased IFNγ expression in T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 9d). Notably, following anti-PD-1 treatment, T reg cells showed upregulated levels of phosphorylated AKT and S6 proteins in tumours, but not in PLNs (Fig. 3g, h). Moreover, SCAP-deficient T reg cells also showed increased levels of phosphorylated AKT and S6 in tumours, further supporting a role for SCAP and PD-1 signalling in shaping PI3K activity in intratumoral Treg cells (Fig. 3i, j). Collectively, these results suggest that SCAP/SREBP and PD-1 repress IFNγ expression and PI3K signalling in intratumoral T reg cells.

To determine the effects of the TME and other contexts on PD-1 expression by Treg cells, we examined PD-1 regulation in different models. PD-1 expression was induced on Treg cells in the TME by comparison with those in the spleen (Fig. 4a). Moreover, expression of Pdcd1 mRNA was upregulated in tumour-infiltrating T reg cells as compared with peripheral T reg cells in public scRNAseq datasets from mouse melanoma models 7,8 (Extended Data Fig. 9e, f) and patients with HNSCC 10 (Fig. 4b) or breast cancer 9 (Extended Data Fig. 9g). However, induction of PD-1 expression by T reg cells was not observed in the EAE model (Fig. 4a), suggesting context-selective regulation. By contrast, expression of CTLA4 and ICOS was upregulated in T reg cells from both tumours and EAE (Extended Data Fig. 9h, i). To determine the upstream signals that regulate PD-1 expression, we carried out transcriptome analysis of PD-1 hi and PD-1 lo T reg cells isolated from the TME. Moreover, PD-1 expression was upregulated on T reg cells in response to in vitro TCR stimulation (Extended Data Fig. 9k), indicating TCR-dependent induction of PD-1 expression. TCR stimulation also upregulated the expression of SREBP gene targets (Extended Data Fig. 9l, m). Notably, deletion of SCAP in Treg cells impaired TCR-induced expression of surface PD-1 (Fig. 4e) and Pdcd1 mRNA in vitro (Extended Data Fig. 9n). Thus, SCAP/SREBP signalling links TCR stimulation to PD-1 expression on Treg cells.

SCAP-deficient T reg cells showed reduced incorporation of [ 13 C2]acetate-derived carbons into cholesterol, as determined by metabolomic tracing analysis (Fig. 4f). Treatment with simvastatin-which targets HMGCR, a rate-limiting enzyme in mevalonate metabolism-inhibited PD-1 expression on TCR-stimulated Treg cells (Fig. 4g). In addition to cholesterol, the mevalonate pathway generates the isoprenoids farnesylpyrophosphate (FPP) and geranylgeranylpyrophosphate (GGPP) 33 -lipid molecules that mediate the post-translational protein modifications of farnesylation and geranylgeranylation via the enzymatic activities of farnesyltransferase (catalytic subunit encoded by Fntb ) and geranylgeranyltransferase type I (catalytic subunit encoded by Pggt1b ), respectively. Therefore, we tested the effects of FPP and GGPP on PD-1 regulation. As expected, mevalonate-the metabolite directly downstream of HMGCR-rescued PD-1 expression on simvastatin-treated T reg cells (Fig. 4h). Notably, GGPP treatment also restored PD-1 expression on these cells, whereas FPP showed only a minor rescue effect (Fig. 4h). Moreover, mevalonate or GGPP treatment also largely rescued the expression of Pdcd1 mRNA in simvastatin-treated T reg cells (Extended Data Fig. 10e). These results suggest a possible role of GGPP-dependent protein geranylgeranylation in promoting PD-1 expression by T reg cells.

In support of this notion, treatment with the geranylgeranyltransferase type I inhibitor GGTI (GGTI-2147) 33 strongly inhibited TCR-induced PD-1 expression on T reg cells, while the farnesyltransferase inhibitor FTI (FTI-277) 33 had a small effect (Extended Data Fig. 10f). Moreover, Treg cells deficient in Pggt1b (Extended Data Fig. 10g), but not Fntb (Extended Data Fig. 10h), showed reduced TCR-induced PD-1 expression in vitro, validating the results using the pharmacological inhibitors. Protein geranylgeranylation promotes signalling via Rac and the activity of AP-1 transcription factors in T reg cells 34 , the latter of which being implicated in PD-1 regulation 35 . Indeed, PD-1 induction upon TCR stimulation was impaired on T reg cells deficient in Rac1 and Rac2 (Extended Data Fig. 10i), or following treatment with AP-1 inhibitors (Extended Data Fig. 10j). Together, these data reveal the metabolic dependence of PD-1 expression by T reg cells, through a signalling axis composed of SCAP/SREBP, mevalonate metabolism, protein geranylgeranylation and downstream Rac signalling.

Treg cells accumulate in tumours and hinder effective antitumour immune responses, and therefore represent a promising target for cancer immunotherapy 2 . However, given the overall importance of Treg cells in maintaining immune homeostasis and tissue integrity 1 , the therapeutic targeting of T reg cells in cancer can also lead to autoimmunity. Emerging studies are uncovering context-specific functional reprogramming of Treg cells in tissues, including the identification of surface receptors involved in this process 22,25 . Recent studies have also revealed mechanisms by which intratumoral T reg cells are functionally reprogrammed to prevent fragility in the TME 25,26 . However, whether and how the functional fitness of T reg cells can be shaped by intracellular metabolic networks in the TME remain largely unknown. By combining unbiased systems approaches and comprehensive genetic tools, we have shown here that intratumoral T reg cells actively rewire SREBP-dependent de novo lipid biosynthesis, which acts to maintain the functional state of T reg cells in the TME. Mechanistically, SREBPs coordinate FASN-dependent fatty-acid synthesis and mevalonate-metabolism-dependent induction of Pdcd1 expression (Extended Data Fig. 10k).

Although co-inhibitory receptors-including PD-1-have previously been implicated in the functional fitness of T reg cells 13,31,36 , the regulation of their expression and the mechanistic effects in intratumoral T reg cells remain poorly understood. We have revealed here a tumour-selective induction of PD-1 expression on T reg cells and defined the metabolic dependence of such regulation, through a previously unknown metabolic signalling axis that comprises SCAP/SREBP, mevalonate metabolism and protein geranylgeranylation, which further links to Rac signalling. We have also shown that SCAP/SREBP signalling and PD-1 function in intratumoral T reg cells are important for the proper control of PI3K signalling and IFNγ production, which are detrimental to the suppressive function of T reg cells 24 , including in the TME 25,26 . Our results also indicate that PD-1 and FASN have discrete effects on the functional fitness of T reg cells, with FASN contributing to the TCR-induced functional maturation of T reg cells but not to IFNγ regulation. From a therapeutic point of view, future studies are warranted to determine the extent to which T reg cells in the TME are sensitive to the inhibition of SREBP and lipid metabolism relative to other cells 37,38 . Collectively, our results establish a metabolic checkpoint in intratumoral T reg cells that enforces functional specialization in the TME, and reveal new cellular and molecular targets for cancer immunotherapy.

## Article Methods

No statistical methods were used to predetermine sample size. The experiments were not randomized and, except in the case of histological analyses, investigators were not blinded to allocation during experiments and outcome assessment.

## Mice

Rag1 -/-, Scap fl/fl , Fasn fl/fl , Rac1 fl/fl , Rac2 -/-, Nur77-GFP, Foxp3 DTR , Foxp3 RFP , Rosa26 YFP reporter (with a loxP -site-flanked STOP cassette followed by the sequence encoding yellow fluorescent protein (YFP) inserted into the Rosa26 locus) and C57BL/6 mice were purchased from the Jackson Laboratory. Foxp3 YFP-Cre and Foxp3 GFP-Cre-ERT2 mice were from A. Rudensky 14,39 . Fntb fl/fl and Pggt1b fl/fl mice were from M. Bergo 40,41 . Hmgcr fl/fl mice were as described 42 . All genetic models were on the C57BL/6 background. Both male and female mice were used for analysis and quantification. Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/fl , Foxp3 Cre Fasn fl/fl and Foxp3 Cre Hmgcr +/fl mice were used at 7-18 weeks old, unless otherwise noted, with age- and sex-matched Foxp3 Cre -containing mice as controls. Foxp3 Cre Hmgcr fl/fl , Foxp3 Cre Fntb fl/fl , Foxp3 Cre Pggt1b fl/fl and Rac1 fl/fl Rac2 -/-mice were used at less than 3 weeks old owing to systemic inflammation, with age- and sex-matched Foxp3 Cre -containing mice as controls. In selective experiments, bone-marrow chimaeras were generated by transferring 5 × 10 6 T-cell-depleted bone-marrow cells into sublethally irradiated (5 Gy) Rag1 -/-mice, to expand the cohort of mice for the desired genotypes. Foxp3 Cre/DTR mosaic mice were treated with diphtheria toxin (50 μg kg -1 ) intraperitoneally three times per week, for a total of four injections. The mice were euthanized, and tissues were harvested for flow-cytometry analysis 11 days following the first treatment with diphtheria toxin 16,17 . All mice were kept in a specific-pathogen-free facility in the Animal Resource Center at St Jude Children's Research Hospital. Animal protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of St Jude Children's Research Hospital.

## Flow cytometry

For analysis of surface markers, cells were stained in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 2% ( w / v ) bovine serum albumin (BSA) and the appropriate antibodies. Surface proteins were stained for 20 min on ice. Intracellular staining was performed with the Foxp3/transcription factor staining buffer set, according to the manufacturer's instructions (eBioscience). For staining of intracellular cytokines, cells were stimulated for 4 h with phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) plus ionomycin in the presence of monensin before being stained according to the manufacturer's instructions using the Cytofix/Cytoperm fixation/permeabilization kit (BD Biosciences).

Staining of active caspase-3 was performed using instructions and reagents from the active caspase-3 apoptosis kit (BD Biosciences). BrdU staining (pulsed for 18 h) was performed using instructions and reagents from the APC BrdU flow kit (BD Biosciences). 7-Aminoactinomycin D (7AAD; Sigma) or fixable viability dye (eBioscience) was used for dead-cell exclusion. The following fluorescent conjugate-labelled antibodies were used: anti-CD4 (RM4-5), anti-CD8α (53-6.7), anti-TCRβ (H57-597) (all from Tonbo Biosciences); anti-CD25 (PC61.5), anti-CD44 (1M7), anti-CD62L (MEL-14), anti-CD45.2 (104), anti-ICOS (C398.4A), anti-PD-1 (J43), anti-CD36 (CRF D-2712) (all from BD Biosciences); anti-Foxp3 (FJK-16 s), anti-IFNγ (XMG1.2), anti-IL-4 (11B11), anti-IL-17 (17B7), anti-GITR (DTA-1), anti-TIM3 (RMT3-23) (all from eBioscience); and anti-CD69 (H1.2F3), anti-CD39 (Duha59), anti-CD73 (TY/11.8), anti-Nrp-1 (3E12), anti-TNFα (MP6-XT22) and anti-CTLA4 (UC10-4B9) (all from BioLegend).

To monitor cell division, lymphocytes were labelled with CTV (Life Technologies). For detection of phosphorylated signalling proteins, lymphocytes were rested in a complete medium for 1 h, and then fixed with Phosflow lyse/fix buffer, followed by permeabilization with Phosflow perm buffer III (BD Biosciences), and staining with antibodies against S6 phosphorylated at Ser 235 and Ser 236 (D57.2.2E) and AKT phosphorylated at Thr 308 (D25E6) (all from Cell Signaling Technology). To measure fatty-acid uptake, intratumoral T reg cells were resuspended in complete medium containing BODIPY FL C12 (Life Technologies) at a final concentration of 0.5 μM and incubated in the dark for 30 min at 37 °C. Cells were then washed twice with PBS containing 2% ( w / v ) BSA to remove staining solution, followed by standard surface staining. For mitochondrial staining, lymphocytes were incubated for 30 min at 37 °C with 10 nM MitoTracker Deep Red (Life Technologies), 20 nM tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester (TMRM; ImmunoChemistry Technologies) or 5 μM MitoSOX Red (Life Technologies) after staining of surface markers 43 . Flow-cytometry data were acquired on a LSRII or LSR Fortessa (BD Biosciences) and analysed using Flowjo software (Tree Star). Expression of TCR-β was used to define CD4 + and CD8 + T cells throughout this study.

## Tumour model and isolation of TILs

MC38 colon adenocarcinoma cells were maintained in the laboratory and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% ( v / v ) fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% ( v / v ) penicillin plus streptomycin. B16-F10 melanoma cells were maintained in the laboratory and cultured in Dulbecco's modified essential medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% ( v / v ) FBS and 1% ( v / v ) penicillin plus streptomycin. Mice were injected subcutaneously with 5 × 10 5 MC38 colon adenocarcinoma cells or 2 × 10 5 B16-F10 melanoma cells in the right flank 43 . Tumours were measured regularly with digital callipers and tumour volumes were calculated by the formula: length × width × [(length × width) 0.5 ] × π/6. Tamoxifen was injected intraperitoneally daily as indicated. Anti-PD-1 antibody (200 μg; clone 29F.1A12) or rat IgG2a isotype control antibody (200 μg; clone 2A3) was injected intraperitoneally four times in 100 μl PBS on days 7, 10, 13 and 16 after injection of tumour cells. Anti-CD8α antibodies (200 μg; clone YTS169.4) were injected in 100 μl PBS on days -1, 2, 5, 8 and 11. To prepare tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) 43 , tumour was excised, minced and digested with 1 mg ml -1 collagenase IV (Roche) plus 200 U ml -1 DNase I (Sigma) for 1 h at 37 °C. TILs were isolated by density-gradient centrifugation over Percoll (Life Technologies).

## EAE induction

Mice were immunized subcutaneously at four injection sites with a total of 200 μl of emulsified incomplete Freund's adjuvant supplemented with 1 mg Mycobacterium tuberculosis strain H37Ra (Difco) (complete Freund's adjuvant; CFA) and 200 μg myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (amino acids 35-55; MOG35-55 ), and received intraperitoneal injections of 200 ng pertussis toxin (PTX; List Biological Laboratories) at the time of immunization and 48 h later 44 . Mice were observed daily for clinical signs and scored as follows: 0, no overt signs of disease; 1, limp tail; 2, limp tail plus hindlimb weakness; 3, total hindlimb paralysis; 4, hindlimb paralysis plus 75% of body paralysis (forelimb paralysis/ weakness); 5, moribund.

## Cell purification and culture

Unless otherwise noted, lymphocytes were isolated from spleen and PLNs (including axillary, inguinal and cervical lymph nodes; the same numbers of PLNs were collected from each mouse), and Treg cells (CD4 + Foxp3-YFP + ) were sorted on a MoFlow (Beckman-Coulter) or Reflection (i-Cyt). Sorted cells were cultured in plates coated with anti-CD3 antibody (2C11, 10 μg ml -1 ) and anti-CD28 antibody (37.51, 10 μg ml -1 ; both from Bio X Cell) for the indicated times in Click's medium (Irvine Scientific) supplemented with β-mercaptoethanol, 10% ( v / v ) FBS, 1% ( v / v ) penicillin plus streptomycin and IL-2 (200 U ml -1 ). In selective experiments, GGTI-2147 (5 μM; Calbiochem), FTI-277 (10 μM; Tocris), cholesterol (4.5 μg ml -1 ; Sigma), simvastatin (2 μM; Cayman Chemicals), Sr11302 (30 μM; Tocris), T-5224 (120 μM; Cayman Chemicals) and BSA-palmitate saturated fatty acid complex (100 μM; Cayman Chemicals) were added to the culture. T reg cells were pretreated with inhibitors for 1 h and then stimulated with 10 μg ml -1 anti-CD3/28 antibodies plus 200 U ml -1 IL-2 in the presence of inhibitors. Mevalonate (500 μM; Sigma), FPP (50 μM; Sigma) and GGPP (5 μM; Sigma) were added to the culture. Cells were harvested for flow-cytometry analysis after three days. In vitro T reg cell suppression assays were performed as described 45 . Briefly, freshly sorted naive CD4 + T cells (TN; CD4 + CD62L + CD44 lo CD25 ; 5 × 10 4 ) and T reg cells (at different ratios with T N cells) were cultured in 96-well plates along with anti-CD3 antibody and irradiated splenocytes for 72 h, followed by thymidine incorporation assays. For T reg cell suppression assays using previously activated T reg cells, T reg cells were activated with anti-CD3/CD28 antibodies plus IL-2 for 3 days in the absence or presence of BSA-palmitate saturated fatty acid complex. Viable T reg cells were purified using Ficoll and incubated with freshly isolated T N cells for additional analysis as described earlier.

## Histology

Tissues were fixed with 10% ( v / v ) neutral buffered formalin solution, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with haematoxylin and eosin; the clinical signs of inflammation were analysed by an experienced pathologist (P. Vogel) 44 .

## Gene-expression profiling and analysis

We carried out microarray analyses (Affymetrix mouse Clariom S assay) of total RNA collected in individual batches from the following samples: first, T reg cells from tumours or PLNs of Foxp3 Cre Scap +/+ or Foxp3 Cre Scap +/fl control ( n = 4 biological replicates) and Foxp3 Cre Scap fl/ fl ( n = 3 biological replicates) mice bearing B16 melanoma; second, T reg cells from tumours of Foxp3 Cre Fasn +/+ or Foxp3 Cre Fasn +/fl control and Foxp3 Cre Fasn fl/fl mice bearing B16 melanoma ( n = 3 biological replicates each group); third, PD-1 hi and PD-1 lo (top and bottom 10%) T reg cells from tumours of Foxp3 RFP mice bearing MC38 colon adenocarcinoma ( n = 4 biological replicates each group); and fourth, T reg cells (from Foxp3 Cre mice) stimulated with anti-CD3/28 antibodies for 0 h and 8 h ( n = 5 biological replicates per time point). For microarray analyses 43 , expression signals were summarized using the robust multiarray average algorithm (Affymetrix Expression Console v1.1), then analysed by differential expression (DE) using the R package limma version 3.34.9. All the plots were generated using the R package ggplot2 version 2.2.1 and gplots version 3.0.3. Heat maps were generated to show the average expression of genes from biological replicates of the same genotype. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was used to estimate the FDR as described 21 . DE genes were defined by a log 2 fold change (FC) of greater than 0.5 with a P value of less than 0.05.

GSEA was performed as described 46 using the Hallmark database or cell-metabolism-related pathways curated from the KEGG and Reactome databases (Supplementary Table 1). For GSEA using manually curated gene signatures from public datasets, a public microarray dataset (GSE61077) 29 was used to generate a 'T reg -cell-specific TCR-dependent gene signature' (genes downregulated in TCR-α-deficient aT reg cells compared with TCR-α-sufficient aT reg cells; FDR of less than 5%). The set of 'SREBP targets' was derived from a public dataset 47 (genes upregulated in SREBP1a- or SREBP2-transgenic mice but downregulated in the absence of SCAP). DE genes generated by comparing Foxp3-YFP + T reg cells from tumours versus PLNs of Foxp3 Cre Scap +/+ or Foxp3 Cre Scap +/fl mice were used for Ingenuity pathway analysis (IPA) of upstream transcriptional regulators 24 . The top 15 upstream transcriptional regulators were plotted (Extended Data Fig. 1c) with their activation z -score and P value indicated.

We normalized the expression level of each gene to 100,000 UMIs per cell and log-transformed them by adding 1 to the expression matrix.

Data visualization . To identify different clusters in intratumoral immune cells, data were further analysed using Seurat and visualized by uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) 49 , which partitioned cells into 22 unsupervised clusters on the basis of their transcriptomes, with a resolution of 0.5 in FindClusters function. Fourteen immune-cell types were further identified on the basis of prior knowledge and markers highly expressed in each unsupervised cluster. For data from the EAE mouse model, T reg cells from the CNS and spleen were visualized by UMAP. The activity of SREBP signalling was calculated by the average expression of all of the target genes in the gene set and colour-coded (from low to high, blue-red) on the UMAP visualization. Curated violin plots that represent the expression level of genes were generated using the VlnPlot function in Seurat R.

## Metabolomic tracing studies

Collection of cell samples . T reg cells were stimulated with anti-CD3/28 antibodies plus IL-2 in the presence of 2 mM [ 13 C2]-sodium acetate for 2 days. Cells were then collected by a brief centrifugation, washed once with ice-cold saline and centrifuged again, and then the tubes with the cell pellets were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen. The cell pellets were stored at -80 °C until metabolites were extracted.

Extraction of hydrophilic metabolites and lipids . To extract hydrophilic molecules, we used an adapted three-phase solvent system to separate hydrophilic metabolites from lipids, proteins and nucleic acids 50 . Briefly, the cell pellets were resuspended in 0.2 ml of ice-cold saline and then 1 ml of chloroform/methanol (2:1, v / v ) was added. Next, the mixture was homogenized using a Bead Ruptor Elite (OMNI International, Kennesaw, GA, USA) for 30 s at 8 m s -1 , and the homogenate was centrifuged for 15 min at 21,000 g at 4 °C. After centrifugation, the upper aqueous phases were transferred into new tubes, frozen on dry-ice and then dried by lyophilization. The lower organic phases were transferred into new tubes and then dried under a stream of nitrogen at room temperature.

Liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry analysis of isotopic labelling . A Vanquish Horizon ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system (Thermo Scientific) was used for the liquid chromatography (LC) separations, using a linear gradient condition (0-1 min 50% B; 1-5.5 min 50% to 99% B; 5.5-8 min 99% B; 8-8.5 min 99% to 50% B; 8.5-10 min 50% B), mobile phase A (water/acetonitrile (6:4, v / v )), and mobile phase B (isopropanol/acetonitrile (9:1, v / v )); both A and B contained 10 mM ammonium acetate. The column used was a Thermo Scientific Accucore C30 (2.1 × 50 mm, 2.6 μm) operated at 40 °C. The flow rate was 250 μl min -1 and the injection volume was 10 μl. All LC/mass spectrometry (MS) solvents and reagents were of the highest purity available (water, acetonitrile, isopropanol and ammonium acetate) purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific. An authentic cholesterol standard was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. A Thermo Scientific Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (QE-MS) equipped with an atmospheric-pressure chemical ionization (APCI) probe was used as the detector. The QE-MS was operated in the positive mode, using a targeted selected ion monitoring method (t-SIM/dd-MS 2 ). The mass spectrometer was operated at a resolution of 140,000 (full width at half maximum (FWHM), at an m / z ratio of 200); automatic gain control (AGC) targeted 2 × 10 5 ; maximum injection time 100 ms; loop count 2; and isolation width 0.5 m / z . For the dd-MS 2 conditions, a resolution of 17,500 was used with AGC targeted 1 × 10 5 ; maximum injection time 50 ms; loop count 2; MS 2 isolation width 0.5 m / z ; normalized condition energy (NCE) 35. The APCI source operating conditions were: sheath gas flow 15; auxiliary gas flow 20; sweep gas 1; discharge current 10 μA; capillary temperature 325 °C; S-lenses RF level 65; vaporizer temperature 325 °C. The ions targeted for cholesterol analysis (parent/daughters m / z ) were 369.3514/215.1792/175.1481.

The data were tabulated as the peak area from each LC/MS chromatography, extracted from the tSIM parent mass (at a mass tolerance of 5 ppm or less). For all of the LC-MS analyses indicated above (with the exception of the t-SIM/dd-MS 2 methods), a wide range of MS 1 spectra scans (full-MS) were also acquired during the entire duration of each chromatography. The full-MS data were acquired at a resolution of 140,000 (FWHM, at m / z 200), using an AGC target of 1 × 10 6 , a maximum injection time of 80 ms and a scan range from 100 m / z to 1,000 m / z . From the actual m / z values of every targeted molecule, exact masses were calculated for every potential labelling proceeding from [ 13 C2]-sodium acetate, and then every potentially labelled m / z value was searched in the full-MS data, at the retention times of their corresponding unlabelled molecules. The data from each potentially labelled molecule were tabulated as the peak areas extracted from the full-MS parent masses (at a mass tolerance of 100 ppm or less). True labelling was assessed in the [ 13 C2]-sodium-acetate-treated samples after carefully determining that the peak areas of every potential labelled m / z were significantly increased when compared with the cold-acetate-treated samples. ## Statistical analysis for biological experiments

For experimental data, we tested the normal distribution, and used Prism 6 software (GraphPad) to analyse data from biological experiments (non-omics data) by performing two-tailed unpaired Student's t -tests. To compare multiple groups, we used one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test. We carried out two-way ANOVA to compare tumour growth curves and EAE disease development curves. Biological replicates were used, unless otherwise noted. P values of less than 0.05 were considered significant. Data are presented as means ± s.e.m.

## Methodology

Sample preparation

Instrument

Software

Cell population abundance

Gating strategy The spleens and peripheral lymph nodes (PLNs) were gently separated under nylon mesh using the flat end of 3-mL syringes. Red blood cells were removed by ACK lysis buffer, followed by washing cells with isolation buffer. After spinning down, the cell pellets were resuspended and filtered with nylon mesh before staining. For staining, cells were placed into 96-well round bottom plates and washed in flow cytometry buffer (PBS containing 2% BSA). For cell sorting, cells were collected in Click's medium with 10% FBS.

<!-- image -->

For the examination tumor infiltrating lymphocytes, tumors were excised, minced and digested with 0.5 mg/ml Collagenase IV (Roche) + 200 UI/ml DNase I (Sigma) for 1 h at 37°C, and then passed through 70 μ m filters to remove undigested tumor. TILs were then isolated by density-gradient centrifugation over Percoll.

LSRII or LSR Fortessa (BD Biosciences); Reflection cell sorter (i-Cyt); MoFlow (Beckman-Coulter).

BD FACSDiva software (LSR II) was used to collect flow cytometry data. Flowjo 9.9.4 or later (Tree Star) was used to analyze flow cytometry data.

The purities of the sorted cells were more than 98%.

For all experiments FSC-A vs. SSC-A gates of the starting cell population were used to identify viable cells. Singlet cells were identified using FSC-A vs. FSC-H gating. Positive populations were determined by the specific antibodies, which were distinct from negative populations. Isotype control was used to distinguish between background and marker-positive events.
